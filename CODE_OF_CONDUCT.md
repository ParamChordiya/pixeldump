# Code of Conduct

## Who this applies to

Everyone who participates in the PixelDump project — contributors, maintainers,
issue reporters, discussion participants, and anyone who interacts with the
community in any project space, online or at events.

---

## What we're building here

PixelDump is a project anyone should feel comfortable contributing to,
regardless of their background, identity, or experience level. The goal is a
space where people can ask questions without embarrassment, disagree without
hostility, and build something good together.

---

## Expected behavior

- Be direct and honest. Say what you mean, mean what you say.
- Critique code and ideas — not people.
- Assume good intent when a message is ambiguous. Ask before reacting.
- If someone is new, meet them where they are. Everyone was new once.
- Accept that your approach won't always be chosen. Disagree, advocate for your
  view, then commit to whatever direction is decided.
- Keep discussions in public channels when they're relevant to others, so the
  whole community benefits.

---

## Behavior that will not be tolerated

- Personal attacks, insults, or contemptuous language directed at any person.
- Harassment of any kind — persistent unwanted contact, threats, or
  intimidation — whether in public threads or private messages.
- Sharing someone's private information (address, employer, contact details)
  without explicit consent.
- Sexualized language or imagery in any project space.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, or community events.
- Any conduct that a reasonable person would recognize as making others feel
  unsafe or unwelcome.

---

## Enforcement

Violations can be reported by emailing **param1111.chordiya@gmail.com**.

Reports will be handled with discretion. The person reported will not be told
who filed the report. All maintainers are expected to respect the
confidentiality of anyone who comes forward.

### What happens after a report

1. **Acknowledgment** — you will receive a reply within 72 hours confirming
   the report was received.
2. **Review** — the maintainer(s) will review the report, gather context if
   needed, and reach a decision.
3. **Action** — one of the responses below will be applied.
4. **Follow-up** — the reporter will be informed of the outcome (without
   sharing details that would identify the reviewed party).

### Response levels

| Level | When it applies | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Reminder** | First-time, minor slip — usually unintentional | Private message explaining the issue and what to do differently |
| **Warning** | Repeated or more significant behavior | Formal warning with a clear description of what must stop; continued behavior escalates |
| **Temporary suspension** | Serious violation or ignoring a warning | Removal from community spaces for a defined period (typically 30–90 days) |
| **Permanent removal** | Pattern of harmful behavior or severe single incident | Permanent ban from all project spaces with no path to reinstatement |

---

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in all project spaces: the GitHub repository
(issues, pull requests, discussions, code review comments), any affiliated chat
or forum, and any in-person or virtual event organized under the PixelDump
name.

It also applies when someone is representing the project publicly — for
example, posting on social media in an official capacity or speaking at a
meetup on behalf of the project.

---

## A note on disagreements

Technical disagreements are healthy and expected. Arguing about architecture,
APIs, naming, or tradeoffs is part of how good software gets built. None of
that falls under this policy — as long as the disagreement stays about the
work, not the person doing it.

If you feel a decision was made unfairly or without proper consideration,
raise it through the normal contribution process (open an issue, leave a
comment, request a discussion). Escalating outside the project's channels is
not the right path.
